One of the biggest frustrations with AI video is unpredictability. You write a prompt, wait, and receive something that is nearly right but drifts in the wrong direction. A useful technique for taming this is to define both the opening and closing images and let the model animate the journey between them. This article explains how that approach works and when to use it.

## The Problem With Open-Ended Generation

When a model invents everything from text alone, many details are left to chance. The camera might move unexpectedly, a product might change shape, or the scene might end somewhere irrelevant to your goal.

For marketing, this randomness is costly. You often know exactly how a clip should begin and end, such as a closed box that opens to reveal a product. Leaving that to chance wastes attempts.

## How Start and End Frames Help

With [first and last frame AI video](https://predis.ai/mcp/seedance/), you supply two images: the starting point and the destination. The model fills in the motion between them. This gives you control over composition, product appearance, and the final message.

## Great Use Cases

Product reveals are a natural fit: a package closed in the first frame and open in the last. Before-and-after transformations work well too, such as a messy room becoming tidy or a plain plate becoming a finished dish. Logo animations and scene transitions also benefit from clear endpoints.

Any clip with a clear beginning and ending state is a candidate.

## Preparing Strong Frames

Image quality directly affects video quality. Use sharp, well-lit images with the same aspect ratio. Keep lighting and perspective similar between frames so the model can interpolate smoothly. Large differences in camera angle or lighting may produce odd motion.

If you generate the frames with an image model first, use consistent prompts for style and color so they match.

## Writing the Motion Prompt

The prompt describes how the scene should change. Keep it concise and concrete: “slow camera push-in as the lid lifts, soft light, smooth motion.” Avoid overloading with competing instructions.

Predis.ai users often keep prompt templates for common motions like reveals, transformations, and slow zooms, which speeds up repeat work.

## Short Clips, Big Impact

Short transitions can be very effective on social platforms. A three-to-five-second reveal can stop the scroll. Combine several short clips in sequence for a longer story, keeping visual style consistent across them.

## Testing and Iteration

Generate a few versions and compare. Check for glitches such as warping, flicker, or objects that change shape mid-motion. If a result is close, adjust the prompt slightly or tweak the frames rather than starting over.

## Planning Your Workflow

Create the start and end images first, then generate the motion. Review the result, add captions or music, and schedule it. Predis.ai helps connect these steps so assets move from idea to published content with less manual handling.

## Quality and Ethics

Review every clip carefully, verify claims, and avoid misleading depictions of products. Follow platform rules for AI-generated content, and be transparent where required.

## Final Thoughts

Defining where a clip starts and ends gives you a level of control that text prompts alone cannot. Prepare clean frames, keep motion prompts simple, and iterate based on what you see. It is an approachable way to make AI video more predictable and more useful for real campaigns.
