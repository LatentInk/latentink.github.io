## Start with the reader, not the word count

“Write a blog post about AI” leaves most of the decisions to the model. It has to guess the audience, the purpose, and the evidence. A better brief gives it those decisions before it writes.

Try this when you have a topic but need help shaping a first draft. Replace the bracketed fields with your own details.

## The prompt

```text
Act as a thoughtful editor helping me draft an article.

Topic: [your topic]
Reader: [who they are and what they already know]
Goal: [what the reader should understand or do afterward]
My main point: [your point of view]
Source material: [paste notes or verified facts]
Voice: clear, direct, and conversational
Length: approximately [number] words

First, identify any information missing from this brief.
Ask up to three questions if the gaps affect the main argument.
Then propose an outline before drafting.

Use only the facts in my source material. Mark any claim that
needs verification with [CHECK]. Do not invent quotes or statistics.
Include a concrete example and end with a useful next step.
```

## Make the first response a conversation

Answer the questions and review the outline. If it feels generic, add a specific reader problem, a personal observation, or a worked example. A distinctive draft usually begins with distinctive input.

## Give feedback that names the problem

Instead of “make it better,” try “the introduction takes too long to explain the problem; open with the example in section two.” You are giving the model a concrete edit to perform.

Before publishing, check every factual claim, replace placeholders, and read the draft aloud. Keep the sentences that sound like something you would actually say.
