A video prompt is a small production brief. Without a clear shot, generated motion can look impressive while failing to communicate the story. Begin with a single moment: who or what is visible, how the camera moves, and what changes during the clip.

## What matters in practice

Google’s October roundup includes new interactive world-simulation work. That is a different concept from a pre-rendered video clip: an interactive world can respond as it runs. Avoid treating a demonstration of one as proof of the other. For ordinary editorial video, the most useful first step remains a storyboard you can review before spending time on generation.

## A workflow to try

1. **Define the purpose.** Explain what the viewer should understand from this one shot.

2. **Constrain motion.** Specify camera movement and subject movement separately; avoid contradictory directions.

3. **Plan continuity.** Record subject appearance, light, location, and transition so adjacent shots can match.

## Try this prompt

```text
Storyboard a short explanatory sequence about [topic].
For each shot, give purpose, subject, framing, camera motion,
subject motion, approximate duration, and transition.
Keep visual continuity explicit. Identify claims that need a
real diagram or verified footage rather than a generated scene.
```

## Before you rely on the result

Check current tool support before specifying duration, audio, or interactive control. Review the entire clip for inconsistent objects and misleading physical behavior. Do not use generated demonstrations as evidence of a real product’s performance.
