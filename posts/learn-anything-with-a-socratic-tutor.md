## Reading an explanation is only the beginning

An answer can feel clear while you are reading it and disappear when you try to use it. A better tutoring conversation gives you a chance to retrieve ideas, make mistakes, and explain your reasoning.

Choose one specific skill, such as understanding JavaScript closures or comparing correlation with causation.

## The prompt

```text
Be my practice partner for [specific topic].
My current level: [what I already understand]
My goal: [a task I want to perform independently]
Available time: [minutes]

Start with one short diagnostic question.
Wait for my answer before continuing.
Ask one question at a time and adapt to my response.
If I struggle, offer a small hint before giving the solution.
Use concrete examples and ask me to explain my reasoning.
Correct mistakes clearly, without pretending my answer is right.
Finish by asking me to solve a new problem without hints,
then summarize what I should practice next.
```

## Make the difficulty useful

If the questions are too easy, ask for an example with one new complication. If you are stuck, explain which step you do understand. That gives the tutor a better starting point than “I don't get it.”

## Check your learning away from the chat

Close the conversation and explain the idea in your own words. Solve a similar problem the next day without help. Use a reliable textbook, documentation, or course to verify definitions and examples, especially in a topic where you cannot yet spot mistakes.

The goal is to become less dependent on the hints over time.
